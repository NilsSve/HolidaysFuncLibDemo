Working Days

View > Working Days shows the library's working-day functions. A working day is a day that is not a weekend day, not an official holiday in the country, and not a day the company is closed.

The view opens on Christmas 2026 in the United Kingdom. Christmas Day is a Friday that year, so the Boxing Day bank holiday moves to Monday 28 December.

Settings

On the left are the library's settings. A change here is used at once by the three functions on the right.

  • Country - the country whose official holidays are days off.
  • UK region - for the United Kingdom only: England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland, which have different bank holidays. This is the piHolidayRegion property.
  • Days off every week - tick the days that are always off. Saturday and Sunday are ticked from the start. This is the psWeekendMask property; the view shows its value below the boxes.
  • The company is closed on - a list of extra days off, such as the days between Christmas and New Year. Use Add and Remove to change the list. This is the padClosedDays property.

1. Is it a working day? (IsWorkingDay)

Enter a date. The answer says whether it is a working day and, when it is not, why: a weekend day, an official holiday (with its name), or a closure day.

2. Add working days (AddWorkingDays)

Enter a date and a number of working days. The answer is the date that many working days later, or earlier when the number is negative. The start date itself is not counted, as Excel's WORKDAY function does not count it: Friday + 1 is Monday.

3. Working days between (WorkingDaysBetween)

Enter two dates. The answer is the number of working days from the first to the second, both dates counted, as Excel's NETWORKDAYS function counts them. The grid below lists every day between the two dates, whether it was counted, and why not when it was not.
