Working days

The working-day functions are for delivery dates, due dates and lead times. A working day is a day that is not a weekend day, not an official holiday in the country, and not a day the company is closed.

  • IsWorkingDay dDate sISO_Short - returns True or False.
  • AddWorkingDays dDate iDays sISO_Short - returns the date iDays working days later, or earlier when iDays is negative. The start date is not counted, as in Excel's WORKDAY: Friday + 1 is Monday.
  • WorkingDaysBetween dStart dEnd sISO_Short - counts the working days from one date to the other, both days counted, as Excel's NETWORKDAYS counts them. It is negative when the end date comes first.

The date ten working days after an order date, in Sweden:

Get AddWorkingDays of ghoCalendarHolidays dOrderDate 10 "SE" to dDueDate

Pass "" as the country to count weekends and closure days only.

Settings

Three properties of ghoCalendarHolidays change what counts as a working day:

  • psWeekendMask - the days off every week, Monday to Sunday, with 1 for a day off. The default "0000011" is Saturday and Sunday; "0000110" is Friday and Saturday.
  • padClosedDays - a list of dates the company is closed, such as the days between Christmas and New Year. For a rule rather than a list, augment the IsClosedDay function.
  • piHolidayRegion - for a country whose holidays differ by region. In the UK the default is England and Wales; BrittishHolidays.pkg defines CI_UKRegionScotland, CI_UKRegionNorthernIreland and CI_UKRegionAny:

Set piHolidayRegion of ghoCalendarHolidays to CI_UKRegionScotland

Only official holidays are days off. A special day, such as Christmas Eve in Sweden, is a working day unless you list it in padClosedDays.

To try the functions and settings before you write any code, use the demo's Working Days view.
